# DEAD STREET: individual faction scores 04

Owner accepted the direction of contrast03 and asked to extend it with close attention to each faction's background. Fifteen new scores; Eastex, Ravicci and Blacktop audio copied exactly. All remain listening previews. No vocals or runtime changes.

Sources: live assets/data/faction_glossary.json at ca7688947ef3ae67bdb136359c43666fe4d99e70; original Major Gangs and Authorities / Possible Merged Factions sheets attached by the owner; docs/FACTION_WORK_ORDER.md. Reference sheets include campaign information intentionally absent from the public glossary. Musical interpretation below does not add faction lore.

| Faction | Background shaping the piece | Individual musical design |
|---|---|---|
| Eastex 44’s | 44th Street gang; Mercer rivalry, McAllister border | Preserve liked slow rap score and chopped keys exactly. |
| Calle Ocho | Homegrown south-side Mexican-American gang, exposed to outside cartel interests | Threatening lowrider rap: hanging muted-guitar answers, bent bass, loose rimshot pocket. Local street sound with uneasy gaps. |
| Ventresca | Established mafia family competing with more powerful Ravicci | Claustrophobic small-room noir; muted trumpet against organ and an uneven five-beat stalking pulse. |
| Ravicci | City's strongest criminal family | Preserve liked exposed piano/cello chamber score exactly. |
| Orlov | Russian mafia with an uncertain mercenary connection | Cold post-punk: persistent picked bass, corroded guitar and dry, straight drums. Concrete pressure rather than army ceremony. |
| Zangyaku | Yakuza, Bìtiān rival, covert Onmitsu division | Fractured industrial guitar: short seven-beat attack groups, sudden cuts and isolated plucks. No flowing melody. |
| Bìtiān | Triad, counterweight to Zangyaku | Suspended nocturnal trip-hop: hollow electric-piano voicings, staggered bass and delayed metallic answers. Restrained, lurking tension. |
| Sierra Roja | Outside cartel's local south-side operation | Low-register guitar and brass in a grim compound pulse; measured threat, with no festive flourishes. |
| McAllister | Railroad fortune, greater wealth than Whittaker, helped create TRC | Baritone blues and cold piano over a lurching rail rhythm; polished control with mechanical weight. |
| Mercer44 | Mercer/Eastex union, often under pressure from the dynasties | Two interlocking bass voices and heavy brass interruptions; a larger hostile street formation, no victory uplift. |
| W-M Corporation | Oil/land/rail merger, Nelly's leadership, combined institutional reach | Slow industrial Southern rock, organ and paired mechanical impacts; consolidated power that keeps tightening. |
| Unión del Sur | Union of homegrown gang and cartel's local network; possible concealed cartel ties | Dry street drums against guitar and low brass; two layers gradually lock into a harder collective pulse. |
| Lombardia | Union of the rival Italian families | Monumental low orchestral blocks, organ and timpani; expanded institutional menace, without copying either family melody. |
| Sand Raiders | Poorly equipped, numerous western marauders; burning, pillage, melee Brutes | Uneven massed floor drums, rattling metal, feedback and abrupt empty spaces. Ragged physical violence. |
| al-Saffar | Waterfront traders; cheap contaminated goods conceal a takeover, later armed reinforcement | Coiled plucked-string phrases, hollow dock-like metallic resonance and a concealed low pulse that emerges late. This is the port trader identity, not desert-raider music. |
| Kurgan | Independent Russian mercenaries pursuing their own interests | Dry measured snare/tom drill, blunt low brass and machine strikes. Controlled, heavy operational menace. |
| Ashford-Crane | Masked thieves, impersonation and deliberately provoked conflict | Broken theatrical chamber miniature; pizzicato/celeste phrases exchange roles and stop before resolution. Deception through changing musical voices. |
| Blacktop | Incoming outlaw club supplying hard drugs, potential Stateline alliance or war | Preserve liked guitar-led doom score exactly. |

Shared infrastructure is limited to instrument rendering and mastering. Each new score owns its phrase lengths, rhythms, bass, harmony and instrumentation. Approximately 80% of audition01 BPM retained. Repetition within one composition is intentional; no universal bass sequence or drum loop is used across factions. Do not treat score differences or file hashes as proof of artistic success.
